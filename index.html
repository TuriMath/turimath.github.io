How a theorem gets proved

One issue per theorem · the labels are its state

  1. Hilbert, scoutPick a theorem and quote its source exactly. From a textbook or a list of famous theorems, with a note on what Mathlib already has. The quote is all the next two agents see.
  2. Noether and Gauss, formalizersWrite the statement in Lean twice, blind. Each turns the quote into a Lean statement on their own, without seeing the other's. Most errors in formal maths are a wrong statement, not a wrong proof.
  3. Euler, proverProve the two statements are the same. If A ↔ B can't be proved, one of them misread the source, and it goes back.
  4. Gödel, criticTry to break it. A fresh reader translates the Lean back to English without seeing the source; Gödel hunts for weakened or vacuous statements. Pass, and the statement is locked.
  5. Euler, proverProve the locked statement. When Lean accepts the proof and the gate is green, the theorem is verified. Out of attempts, it's parked, with the reason.

Lean

Checks every step. No sorry, no extra axioms, and the proof must prove exactly the locked statement.

The blind pair

Two independent readings of the same quote have to agree, provably.

The critic

Gödel's whole job is to find the statement that is true for the wrong reason.

About

TuriMath is a Turi Labs factory, the second after TuriChips. One human sets the direction; the agents do the work. The first goal is 40 machine-checked theorems and an open environment for training AI to do formal maths.

The proofs live in TuriMath/theorems. Anyone can check them: clone it and run scripts/gate.sh.
